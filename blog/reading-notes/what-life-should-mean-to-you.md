# What Life Should Mean to You

> Alfred Adler

## The Influence of Family

### The Mother's Influence

- Most important at the beginning of an infant's life.
- A child continually seeks the mother's attention.

### The Father's Role and Responsibilities

- The relationship is not as close at first; the father's influence comes later than the mother's and can be greater.
- A child may worship the father as an idol or regard him as the greatest enemy.
- When the father gets along well with his own parents and siblings, it benefits the child's capacity to cooperate.

### Relationships Between Family Members

- The eldest child usually receives a great deal of attention and becomes accustomed to being the center of the family.
- The second child faces a rival from birth and finds it easier to cooperate with others.
- The youngest child has the most rivals and may be more likely to surpass the other children in achievement.

## The Influence of School

### The School's Responsibilities

- School is an extension of the family, and the teacher's work resembles the mother's role.
- Both skipping a grade and being held back do more harm than good.

### Cooperation and Competition in the Classroom

- Children prefer competition to cooperation.
- Even without examinations, they know one another's abilities.
- They become more eager to learn when they receive recognition or praise.

## Childhood and Early Memories

### Childhood's Influence on Life

- **Physical impairments:** among children who experience pressure because of an organ impairment, more go on to struggle or fail.
- **Pampering:** spoiled children may grow into a dangerous group.
- **Neglect:** neglected children are not good at relating to others and often overlook the possibility of cooperation.

### Early Memories

- Memories reveal a person's secrets more clearly than anything else; they are not accidental.
- People remember events that are especially important to their circumstances.
- Early memories are the most revealing: they can show a person's fundamental outlook on life.

## Adolescence

### Psychological Characteristics

- Wanting to prove that one is no longer a child.
- Seeing adolescence as the end of everything.

### The Aims of Behavior

- Demonstrating independence.
- Demanding equality with adults.

### The Struggles of Adolescence

- Feeling intense fear because one is poorly prepared for adult life.
- Most who struggle in adolescence were pampered as children.

## Mind and Body

### The Interaction Between Mind and Body

- Body and mind form an indivisible whole.
- The mind governs the body and anticipates the direction of behavior.

### The Importance of Mental Development

- The mind can also influence the brain.
- One's way of life and tendencies toward psychological complexes continually affect the body.

## Feelings of Inferiority and Superiority

### The Inferiority Complex

- **When it appears:** feelings of inferiority are more likely to arise when a person cannot solve a problem.
- **How it appears:** anger, tears, tension, and apologies.
- Feelings of inferiority are not inherently pathological; they are a reason that human beings strive to improve their position.

### Striving for Superiority

- The pursuit of superiority is common to all human beings.
- Everyone has a goal of superiority, and that goal is unique to the individual.
- People in states of mental derangement often display their goals of superiority.

## The Meaning of Life

### Three Realities of Human Life

1. We cannot live apart from the surface of the Earth.
2. We are not the only members of humanity; we must form connections with other people.
3. Humanity has two sexes, and the continuation of individual and collective life depends on this.

### Social Feeling

- For the individual, work, social relationships, and sexuality encompass all the problems of life.
- Almost everyone who fails lacks a sense of belonging and social interest.
- Learning to cooperate is an important safeguard against pathological development.

## Work

### Early Preparation for Work

- The mother is the first person to influence the development of a child's vocational interests.
- The second stage of vocational preparation takes place at school.

### Other Factors That Influence Career Choices

- Early effort provides the best foundation for success.
- People want to surpass their parents and other family members.
- Love and social problems also play an important role in shaping one's working life.

## Love and Marriage

### The Importance of Love and Marriage

- Love and marriage are both aspects of cooperation.
- One must learn how to act without angering one's partner.

### Prerequisites for Love and Marriage

- Mutual attraction.
- A shared wish to have and raise children.
- An equal partnership.

## The Interpretation of Dreams

*Discussed in greater detail in The Interpretation of Dreams.*

### Interpreting Dreams

- **The Freudian school of psychoanalysis:** often goes beyond the bounds of science.
- **Individual psychology:** bases its interpretations on common sense.

### Characteristics of Dreams

- Dreams are composed mainly of metaphors and symbols.
- Our personality is the same when we are dreaming as when we are awake.
- The purpose of dreams is self-deception and self-indulgence.
- We usually forget a dream after waking, but its feelings remain.

## Crime and Its Prevention

### Criminal Psychology

- Crime arises from the pursuit of security.
- Criminals lack interest in others and have only a limited capacity for cooperation.

### Correcting Criminal Behavior

- Criminal behavior is also an extension of human behavior.
- Identify the obstacles to cooperation that the offender faced in childhood.
- Parents' complaints in front of their children can hinder the development of the children's social interest.
