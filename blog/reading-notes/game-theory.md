# Game Theory

> John von Neumann

## Basic Elements of a Game

- **Decision-maker:** the person who makes the first choice.
- **Opponent:** the person who chooses later.
- **Biological affinity for order:** a tendency for organisms to actively seek and approach order.
- **Players:** the participants in a game.
- **Strategies.**
- **Gains and losses:** determined by a function of the combination of strategies chosen by the players—the payoff function.
- **Order:** when the order of decisions changes, the outcome may change even if the other elements remain the same.

## Defining Game Theory

- Mathematical analysis requires a complete axiomatic system. At the end of the chain of logical reasoning, mathematical symbols must be translated back into things in the real world.
- Game outcomes and systems for attributing responsibility.

## Types of Games

### Binding Agreements

- **Cooperative games:** binding agreements are possible.
- **Non-cooperative games:** no binding agreements; common in economics.

| Game type | Equilibrium concept |
| --- | --- |
| Static game with complete information | Nash equilibrium |
| Static game with incomplete information | Bayesian Nash equilibrium |
| Dynamic game with complete information | Subgame-perfect Nash equilibrium |
| Dynamic game with incomplete information | Perfect Bayesian equilibrium |

### Timing of Decisions

- **Static games:** players who act later do not know the earlier players' choices. Example: the prisoner's dilemma.
- **Dynamic games:** examples include board games and card games.

### Available Information

- Games with complete information.
- Games with incomplete information.

### Number of Rounds and Duration

- Finite games.
- Infinite games.

### Representation

- Strategic-form games.
- Extensive-form games.
